## 1. A Clear and Communicated AI Stance

The absence of a clear policy doesn't create neutrality. It creates two distinct failure modes.

The first: agents who are too conservative. They won't use AI tools for anything that feels ambiguous, which is most things. They're afraid of overstepping. So they ignore tools that would make them faster, better, more accurate - and you wonder why adoption stalls.

The second: agents who are too permissive. They use AI everywhere, including places that are unapproved, risky, or outside compliance boundaries. They're not malicious. They just filled the vacuum you created.

A good AI stance uses three buckets:

- **Prohibited** - specific use cases that are off-limits and why
- **Permitted with Guardrails** - approved with defined constraints (review, logging, oversight)
- **Actively Encouraged** - this is where you're betting on the technology and want maximum adoption

This document needs to be cross-functional - Legal, Security, Operations, and Workforce together - and it needs to be a living document, not a policy PDF that ages out in a drawer. Socialize it widely. Reference it in onboarding. Review it quarterly.

A clear stance doesn't slow people down. It removes friction. When agents know exactly what's sanctioned, they move faster and more confidently. Ambiguity is the tax on productivity. Clarity is the multiplier.

---

## 2. A Healthy Data Ecosystem

AI amplifies what's already there. That's the value proposition. It's also the risk.

In support, your AI is only as good as the data it touches: your knowledge base, ticket history, escalation records, account health data, product documentation. Most support orgs have data that is siloed by team or tool, outdated because no one owns freshness, incomplete because it was never built for machine consumption, and inaccessible because it lives in six different systems with no unified layer.

When AI connects to bad data, it doesn't produce worse answers at the rate a human would. It produces bad answers at scale, with confidence, faster than you can catch them. A support agent referencing a stale knowledge base article is a one-off problem. AI recommending that stale answer to every similar ticket is an incident.

DORA's research makes a point that practitioners often miss: without healthy data, AI creates localized productivity gains that get absorbed by downstream bottlenecks. An agent resolves faster, but the next system in the chain - billing, engineering, logistics - hasn't changed. The bottleneck shifts. Nothing improves end-to-end.

The right starting move: pick one data source. Make it clean, current, owned, and accessible. Pilot AI against that source only. Measure what changes. Then scale. Don't try to boil the ocean. Fix one data stream and prove the model before you expand it.

---

## 3. User-Centric Focus

Back to the finding that should be keeping support leaders up at night.

DORA found that user-centric focus predicts a **40% improvement in organizational performance**. And teams that adopt AI without it see performance go down.

In support, the question is simple: are you measuring tickets closed, or problems solved?

These are not the same metric. One measures throughput. The other measures outcomes for the customer. Teams optimized for throughput can close tickets at high velocity while customer problems persist, escalate, and churn. AI in a throughput-focused org just means you close tickets even faster while solving fewer problems.

AI accelerates movement in whatever direction the team is already pointed. If that direction is the right one - genuine resolution, customer outcomes, problem elimination - AI makes you dramatically more effective. That's the 45% capacity increase I saw without adding a single headcount.

If the direction is wrong, AI makes it worse, faster. You will hit the ceiling on the wrong metric with extraordinary efficiency.

Before deploying AI, ask your team to define what "success" looks like for a customer interaction. If the answer is "ticket closed," that's your real problem. AI is not the fix for that. Leadership is.

## The Foundation Comes First

These three capabilities - a clear stance, healthy data, and user-centric focus - aren't prerequisites you check off once. They're ongoing organizational disciplines. The orgs that get AI deployment right treat them as infrastructure, not prerequisites.

Get the foundation right, and AI compounds. Shortcut the foundation, and AI accelerates your existing problems.
